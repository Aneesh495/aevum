# Exposure alignment and sign reversal

A population's age-band central rate equals total deaths divided by total person-years. Writing this as a weighted mean of single-age rates reveals the weights Eₐ / ΣEₐ. An equal-weight mean describes an age distribution with the same exposure at every age. It is sensitive to how the open interval is split. The difference can propagate through survival and remaining years, even for modest disparities at older ages.

Across the 4,536 WPP 2024 periods for 42 countries and both sexes in 1970–2023, equal weighting changes period e0 by a median absolute 0.183 years. For Norwegian females in 1987–1988 the equal-weight change is +0.021 years, while compatible weighted aggregation gives −0.011. These are deterministic sensitivity contrasts in deaths per person-year across 20 forecast intervals. They do not measure statistical significance. [Evidence and raw run identity](CLAIMS.md)
